# Clinical Disclaimer & Statement of Limitations

**Read this before using Sorbed on any real patient image.**

## Not a medical device

Sorbed is open-source **research and decision-support** software. It is **not**
a medical device and has **not** been reviewed, cleared, or approved by the U.S.
Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the European Medicines Agency (EMA), the UK
Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA), or any other
regulatory body. It must not be marketed or used as a diagnostic device.

## Decision support, not diagnosis

Every grade, measurement, mask, and statistic Sorbed produces is an **estimate
generated by algorithms operating on a two-dimensional image**. Sorbed does not:

- Examine the patient
- Palpate tissue or assess temperature, firmness, or pain
- Measure true wound depth, undermining, or tunneling (these are not recoverable
  from a single 2D photograph)
- Know the patient's history, comorbidities, or care context

A pressure injury stage is a **clinical determination** made by a qualified
professional. Sorbed's output is one input to that determination — never a
substitute for it.

## Known limitations you must account for

- **Skin tone bias.** Stage 1 (non-blanchable erythema) and Deep Tissue Pressure
  Injury are defined partly by color changes that are substantially harder to
  detect in darkly pigmented skin. Automated color analysis inherits and can
  amplify this bias. Sorbed surfaces a lower confidence and an explicit warning
  in these cases, but **a negative or low-stage result on dark skin does not rule
  out injury.**
- **Depth is inferred, not measured.** Distinguishing Stage 3 from Stage 4, or
  identifying unstageable wounds, depends on depth and structures that a photo
  cannot reliably convey.
- **Scale depends on calibration.** Area and length measurements are only as
  accurate as the pixel-to-millimeter calibration supplied (DICOM pixel spacing,
  a fiducial marker, or a manual scale). Uncalibrated images yield relative, not
  absolute, measurements.
- **Image quality matters.** Lighting, white balance, focus, angle, and occlusion
  (dressings, hair, shadows) all affect results.
